Existing systems foundation

I am not starting from zero.

My interest in technology also predates my management career. I began college studying Computer Science at East Carolina University before transferring to Penn State and changing academic direction.

Today, my day-to-day work already involves computerized point-of-sale, inventory, ordering, reporting, scheduling, payroll, and recordkeeping systems. I regularly work with counts, audits, discrepancies, operational reports, and the information needed to keep a store accurate and controlled.

I have also used NCR point-of-sale systems, PDI Retail Site Management, Microsoft Excel, Word, Outlook, and other digital business tools. That is not the same as formal IT experience, but together with my earlier coursework it gives me a more credible technical foundation than simply starting from scratch.

Learning by doing

This website is part of the process.

Building mattkwalker.com has required working with a custom domain, DNS records, HTTPS, GitHub Pages, HTML, CSS, deployment, metadata, and basic web infrastructure.

That does not make me an IT professional or software engineer. It gives me a practical way to learn how technical pieces fit together instead of only reading about them.

I want more of that kind of learning: small, useful systems that teach me something real and compound into stronger technical judgment over time.

What I'm not doing

Avoiding fake progress.

I am not trying to collect random certifications, build projects I do not care about, or present myself as more technical than I am.

The standard is usefulness: learn skills that connect to real work, document what I actually understand, and keep moving toward roles where operations knowledge and technical capability reinforce each other.
